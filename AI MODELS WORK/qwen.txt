### 2. THE ASSISTANT DOSSIER: "THE SHADOW GUIDE"
**Name:** Leon 
**Role:** 27-year-old local barista, community center volunteer, and your first friend.
**Psychological Profile:** Leon is pragmatic, deeply empathetic, and uses humor to defuse tension. His grandparents were *Gastarbeiter* (guest workers); he understands the immigrant struggle intimately but refuses to coddle you. He knows that true fluency requires friction.

#### The 3-Phase Interaction Architecture:

**Phase 1: The Crutch (A0 ➔ Early A1)**
*   **Behavior:** Leon is physically present or constantly on the phone. He acts as your buffer. 
*   **Mechanics:** He translates NPC dialogue in real-time. He points at objects, providing the German noun and article. If you freeze in a conversation, Leon smoothly steps in to finish your sentence.
*   **Pedagogical Purpose:** Lowers the affective filter. Prevents early-game frustration and quit-rate. Builds a foundational receptive vocabulary.

**Phase 2: The Scaffolding Withdrawal (Late A1 ➔ A2)**
*   **Behavior:** Leon starts setting boundaries. "I'll wait outside the *Bäckerei*. You order the bread."
*   **Mechanics:** The UI translation fades to a subtle glow. If you fail an interaction, Leon doesn't immediately rescue you; he lets you experience the social awkwardness, then debriefs you afterward ("*Remember, you have to say 'Ich hätte gern', not 'Ich will'*"). If you freeze for >10 seconds, he sends a subtle in-game text hint.
*   **Pedagogical Purpose:** Forces active recall. Transitions the player from receptive to productive skills. Introduces the fear of "losing face" as a motivational driver.

**Phase 3: The Peer (B1 ➔ C1)**
*   **Behavior:** Leon is just your friend. He speaks at native speed, uses colloquialisms (*Umgangssprache*), and gets visibly annoyed if you speak to him like a textbook.
*   **Mechanics:** No UI hints. If you use overly formal German with him, he mocks you playfully. You must use context clues to understand his slang. You must navigate conflicts with him (e.g., he forgot to pay his share of the internet bill) using complex negotiation and emotional vocabulary.
*   **Pedagogical Purpose:** Achieves true fluency. The player must process natural, messy, un-scaffolded German and respond with appropriate register and pragmatics.

### 4. SPATIAL PROGRESSION MAP & INTERACTIVE ONTOLOGY

The 3D world is divided into zones. Every object is a "language trigger." Interacting with them builds neural pathways through physical association.

#### Zone 1: Die Wohnung (The Apartment) - *The Sanctuary*
*   **Bedroom:** *Bett, Wecker, Lampe, Kleiderschrank, Kleidung.*
    *   *Triggers:* Dative prepositions for location (*Ich schlafe **im** Bett*). Accusative for dressing (*Ich ziehe **den** Mantel an*).
*   **Kitchen:** *Kühlschrank, Herd, Spüle, Müll, Lebensmittel.*
    *   *Triggers:* Partitive concepts (*Ich habe **noch** Milch*). Imperatives for recipes (*Rühr **um**!*). Separable verbs (*Ich **räume** die Spülmaschine **ein***).
*   **Bathroom:** *Spiegel, Zahnbürste, Dusche, Handtuch.*
    *   *Triggers:* Reflexive verbs (*Ich **wasche mich**. Ich **kämm**e **mir** die Haare*).

#### Zone 2: Der Kiez (The Neighborhood) - *The Social Web*
*   **Street/Sidewalk:** *Ampel, Zebrastreifen, Mülleimer, Briefkasten, Hund.*
    *   *Triggers:* Modal verbs of obligation/permission (*Ich **darf** bei Grün gehen. Ich **muss** den Müll trennen*). Two-way prepositions (*Der Hund läuft **um** den Baum*).
*   **Bäckerei / Supermarkt:** *Theke, Kasse, Tüte, Wechselgeld.*
    *   *Triggers:* Polite requests (*Ich hätte gern...*). Numbers and currency. Plural nouns (*drei Äpfel, keine Eier*).
*   **U-Bahn Station:** *Fahrkartenautomat, Gleis, Fahrplan, Verspätung.*
    *   *Triggers:* Time expressions (*Der Zug kommt **um** 8 Uhr **und** 20*). Passive voice for announcements (*Die Tür **wird** geschlossen*).

#### Zone 3: Die Stadt & Die Ämter (City & Bureaucracy) - *The Labyrinth*
*   **Bürgeramt / Bauamt:** *Schalter, Formular, Stempel, Termin, Sachbearbeiter.*
    *   *Triggers:* Formal address (*Sie*). Passive voice (*Das Formular **muss** ausgefüllt **werden***). Complex subordinate clauses (*Ich bin hier, **weil** ich mich anmelden **muss***).
*   **Office / Workspace:** *Schreibtisch, Computer, Besprechung, Frist, Projekt.*
    *   *Triggers:* Professional vocabulary. Konjunktiv II for politeness (*Könnten Sie mir die Datei schicken?*). Genitive prepositions (*Wegen **des** Wetters*).

### DESIGNER'S NOTE ON PEDAGOGICAL PHILOSOPHY
The genius of *Ein Neues Leben in Deutschland* lies in its weaponization of **mundanity**. Textbooks teach "How to buy a train ticket" as a sterile dialogue. In our game, the player must buy a train ticket while it is raining, while their phone battery is at 2%, while a local is standing behind them sighing impatiently. 

By embedding language acquisition into the physical, emotional, and spatial realities of daily life, we bypass the translation center of the brain. The player doesn't learn that *der Kühlschrank* means "the fridge." They learn that *der Kühlschrank* is the cold, humming box they must open every single morning to get the milk before they can start their day. 

They don't study German. They **live** it.

### DESIGNER'S MANDATE FOR THE DEVELOPMENT TEAM:
This is the baseline. If a player decides to look at the baseboards in the hallway, they must see the dust. If they decide to listen to the fridge, they must hear the compressor. We are not building a game; we are building a digital terrarium of German mundane reality. Do not summarize. Do not skip. Give them every single, boring, beautiful, frustrating detail.
