# DRJA: MUNDWERK  
## A zero-wait, cosmic multiplayer race from A0 to A1

**The answer: the summit is reached in exactly 30 floating platforms.**  
Thirty is ideal: six acts of five platforms, each act taking roughly 5–7 minutes. A complete first ascent is a 25–35 minute event; every fifth platform is a safe checkpoint.

The fantasy is simple:

> In this universe, German is not studied. German makes matter.

A correctly shaped sound turns on a lamp. A number catches a meteor. A sentence builds a bridge. By the summit, the player has not “passed a lesson”—they have literally constructed a city in the sky with their first German.

## The north star

`Zn9a street → sealed garage → blackout → lamp → reality tear → 30 Drja → A1 city`

This is a party game first, a language course second, and a memory machine underneath both.

Its non-negotiable rules:

- Every player moves at their own speed; nobody waits for a slow player.
- Every platform is a loud, physical, funny spectacle—not a quiz screen.
- Faces and mouths create comedy, but never humiliation.
- Speech, webcam, controller, and text paths are equally valid.
- Speed earns flair; learning earns progress. No one is eliminated.

## The Zn9a lobby: “the street that knows a secret”

The lobby is a small, lived-in Moroccan urban corner at blue hour: repaired wiring, warm café light, tea-glass clinks, worn shutters, scooters passing somewhere out of frame, and one old industrial garage that looks permanently closed.

It should feel specific and human, made with Moroccan visual and sound collaborators—not like a generic “exotic market” backdrop.

Players spawn through a visible **Paint Lottery Pipe**. A glob of color drops onto each marshmallow bean and assigns:

- a random bright body color;
- a unique constellation symbol;
- a pattern for color-blind readability.

There is no character creator. The absurdity begins immediately: eight random beans with giant live eyes and mouths blinking at each other outside a mysterious garage.

“READY” is physical. Players step onto a chalk circle or press a brass garage remote. Their menu card folds itself into a paper strip and gets sucked beneath the garage door. When the final player is ready, every remaining interface element slides away toward the shutter.

## Opening cinematic: “The Unhousing”

This should run about 15 seconds, remain partially playable, and become the game’s signature short-form-video moment.

| Beat | Picture | Sound / interaction |
|---|---|---|

| 0:04 | The door rumbles open by itself. Warm street light spills across an empty concrete garage. | Chain rattle, hollow reverb. |
| 0:06 | Players enter. Camera performs a slow 90° orbit, hiding the street behind them. | The street sound is replaced by electrical buzz. |
| 0:08.5 | The heavy garage door slams shut. | Exactly one second of pitch-black silence. No UI click, no music, no rumble. |
| 0:09.5 | A single old industrial lamp hangs above the players. One player pulls its chain. | Filament sputter; the player becomes the run’s “Spark.” |
| 0:10–0:12 | For two full seconds: raw walls, dust, bare floor, huge ridiculous faces under warm yellow light. Nothing else. | Gentle lamp hum and tiny concrete-room sounds. |

If nobody pulls the lamp after a few seconds, it self-ignites. The moment never deadlocks.

The tone evokes a reality rupture, but it should be visually and sonically original: no shot-for-shot imitation of another show. The emotional effect is “the world has just been un-housed.”

## The comedy engine: the Big Face

Every bean has an oversized curved face-mask: real eyes and mouth, magnified to absurd proportions and stabilized so they remain readable at a distance.

Three equal modes exist:

1. **Live Big Face** — opt-in webcam texture for private friend groups.
2. **Face Puppet** — local face tracking drives a stylized animated mouth and eyes.
3. **Cosmic Mask** — no camera; players control an expressive puppet with buttons, touch, or keys.

The game never grades attractiveness, facial anatomy, emotion, or identity. Face tracking is a performance controller; pronunciation checks use tolerant audio recognition and accessible alternatives.

A near-correct “Ü” creates a friendly holographic mouth guide. A wrong answer never produces a red X. It produces a wobble, a funny physics reaction, and one useful coaching cue: “Round your lips more,” or “Try the soft `ch` from `ich`.”

## Core match loop

Each match is an **Ascent Cell** of 2–8 players.

Every platform has two layers:

- **Public spectacle:** shared arena, running, bouncing, emotes, giant faces, shared sky effects.
- **Personal Reality Ring:** each player’s own language objects and adaptive prompt set.

Players can laugh together and help one another, but cannot steal an answer, block an object, push another player, or solve a platform for someone else.

Every platform follows this rhythm:

1. Hear or see a German target.
2. Solve it through physical play.
3. Speak, match, or build the language.
4. Get a rocket-bounce launch to the next Drja.

A miss resets only that player’s local object. No falling down the tower, no lives, no public wrong-answer log, no waiting.

After repeated misses, the game offers an assisted route and marks that language item for a later 30-second remix practice. Momentum is protected without pretending the learner mastered something they did not.

# Multiplayer: everyone is a comet, nobody is dead weight

## On the same platform

Players have soft, squishy collisions. They can bounce into one another comically but never shove, block, steal, or sabotage.

Shared social mechanics:

- **Mouth Mirror:** a struggling player can request a two-second mouth-shape hologram from a willing friend or an animated articulation puppet.
- **Chorus Pads:** optional group repetition makes nebulae bloom and improves spectacle, but never locks anyone into a group task.
- **Phrase Wheel:** emotes gradually become useful German: `Hallo!`, `Bitte!`, `Danke!`, `Super!`, `Noch einmal!`
- **Hype Field:** cheering, dancing, and eye-widening fill a retrying player’s courage meter, granting a clearer hint or calm retry—not a free answer.
- **Micro-toys:** players can collect phoneme fireflies or bounce on resonance drums while their friends solve their own private challenge.

The social rule is:

> Friends can reveal technique. Only the learner can earn the launch.

## Helping players below

The vertical minimap is called **The Skyline**. It shows all 30 rungs, every player’s color, symbol, name, and current platform number.

A player who clears a step earns limited **Starlight**, used only for positive support.

A player below can raise a Help Beacon:

- “Show mouth shape”
- “Help me hear it”
- “Where do I look?”
- “I need a calm retry”

Higher players can send:

- **Echo Hologram:** a mouth-shape loop or safe animation guide.
- **Technique Comet:** “Round your lips,” “Listen for the long sound,” or another approved cue.
- **Trail Star:** a temporary visual path toward the relevant object.
- **Mint-Tea Comet:** clears clutter, steadies the camera, and gives a calming breath beat.
- **Zellige Star:** a harmless encouragement tile that becomes floor art.

For shouted advice, use opt-in **Echo Tubes**: 2–3 second captioned voice bursts traveling down the tower. Public rooms default to preset advice rather than open voice.

Fast finishers enter **Observer Orbit**, a balcony view of earlier platforms. They can cheer and help, but cannot interfere or complete tasks for anyone.

## Race pressure without cruelty

The Skyline creates urgency, but never shame:

- Your dot is large and leaves a comet trail on launch.
- The leader gets a temporary Front-Runner Flare, not permanent bragging rights.
- There is no “last place” banner.
- Classroom and co-op modes can collapse the ranking into one shared altitude meter.
- Checkpoints save permanently every five platforms.
- Disconnecting resumes exactly where the player left off.

# Viral without violating trust

The lobby contains a **Café Telescope**: consented friends can watch a player’s ascent from the Zn9a street through a portal window.

The game can detect a “Mouthquake Moment”—a huge `Ö` face, an absurd physics bounce, a difficult sound cleared—but it never auto-records or auto-posts anything.

The player chooses:

- delete;
- save privately;
- share with the party;
- export a stylized postcard or masked-avatar clip.

Raw face footage remains opt-in, party-scoped, and never used for face recognition, emotional profiling, model training, or public sharing by default.

# Summit climax: A0 Signal — Das Tor

Platform 30 is a circular black-glass disc suspended above a dawn-colored void. Five dormant relay nodes surround an enormous door shaped like the original garage shutter.

Each player completes the finale individually. No podium, no waiting room, no need for every friend to arrive.

When the five nodes are restored:

1. Pronunciation, number, greeting, noun, and verb beams ignite around the player.
2. The old industrial lamp rises from the floor beneath them.
3. Its filament becomes a miniature sun.
4. Every completed platform below lights up in sequence, turning the entire staircase into a vertical sentence written through space.
5. The player says or selects: `Hallo, ich heiße ___. Ich lerne Deutsch.`
6. Their enormous face becomes a majestic, ridiculous constellation key: glowing eyes as star poles, mouth tracing the final route through the sky.
7. The garage-door portal opens.

Beyond it is **Morgenstadt**, the A1 world: a warm elevated city of apartments, cafés, transit lines, street signs, windows, people, and actual conversations.

The camera performs the inverse of the opening: instead of a garage becoming infinite space, infinite space becomes a place where the learner belongs.

The player rocket-bounces through the portal and lands in an A1 arrival square as the lamp-sun rises.

On screen:

> **Willkommen in A1. Du hast die Tür geöffnet.**  
> Welcome to A1. You opened the door.

A party that reaches the summit together can unlock an optional shared constellation tableau. Individual completion never depends on it.

## What remains after the summit

The original Zn9a garage changes permanently after each act:

- a brighter lamp filament;
- a number constellation on the ceiling;
- new bilingual signs;
- three article-moons visible behind the shutter;
- a living verb machine inside the wall;
- a thin line of dawn beneath the garage door.

The garage becomes a living museum of what the player can now say.

That is the final emotional image: the ordinary shutter that once looked dead is glowing from within—not because there is more homework behind it, but because an entire A1 world is waiting.
