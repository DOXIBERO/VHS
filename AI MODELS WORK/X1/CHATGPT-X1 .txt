## DAS HAUS VOR DEM HALLO  
*The House Before Hello*

Between two train doors exists a hidden arrival-house made from first sentences that newcomers have not yet said. Its resident is **Punkt**: a palm-sized, springy ink-dot with little legs—cute, mute, and literally incomplete.

Punkt wakes with a copper postcard whose final line is broken:

> `___ bin hier.`

The postcard’s memories have scattered into square **Bildkarten**: tactile photo/art boards. Every room wall contains **Hohlwörter**—German words and sentence fragments hollowed out of reality. Restore the right visual memory to a Hohlwort, and the room remembers a piece of ordinary German life.

The mystery is who sent the card and why it knows the player’s name. The finale reveals it is a welcome from a former newcomer, rebuilt for the next person beginning from zero. Punkt becomes the dot over the `i` in **Ich**, and the completed card reads: **Ich bin hier.**

### Why exactly 14 rooms

A0 is the runway into A1, not a formal CEFR level itself. Fourteen is the smallest honest structure: 13 non-compressible foundations plus one transfer room that proves the learner can actually cope when they do not understand something.

It teaches roughly 450 high-frequency words and 70 reusable frames. It deliberately excludes past tense, formal declension tables, genitive, complex adjective endings, and subordinate clauses—those belong in A1.

The central invention is **semantic physics**: a board never simply matches a picture to a word. German changes its physical state first—gender becomes a material skin, plurals become mitosis, verb position becomes tension, modal verbs become weather, and negation becomes photographic darkness.

## The 14 rooms

### 1. Mundabdruck-Schleuse — Sound and spelling

- **Wall and boards:** Recesses read `Maus`, `Mütze`, `Buch`, `ich`, `Straße`, `Hallo`, and `bitte`. Boards show a mouse, knitted cap, book, child, rain street, and greeting scenes.

- **New interaction — Mouthprint sculpting:** Each board becomes a soft vibrating sound-object. Stretching it reveals long versus short vowels; tilting exposes `u/ü`, while tiny tremors reveal `ch`, `sch`, `z`, and `ei/ie`. Native audio responds as if the board is a playable instrument. Optional speech input gives a brighter resonance.

- **Why it works:** German sound is learned through hands, ears, and rhythm before any grammar appears. The concrete wall accepts the board only once its sound-print has been experienced correctly.

- **Transformation:** Raw plaster cools into glowing phonetic veins; their combined hum opens the first exit.

### 2. Höflichkeits-Orbit — Greetings, farewells, and `du` / `Sie`

- **Wall and boards:** `Guten Morgen` receives a sunrise commuter board; `Guten Tag` a shop-entry board; `Tschüss` a departing friend board; `Wie heißen Sie?` a formal visitor board; `Ich heiße Punkt` Punkt’s name badge.

- **New interaction — Social gravity:** Soft visitor silhouettes move through the empty room with a time-of-day shadow and a personal-space orbit. The player releases phrase boards into those orbits. A formal greeting has a wider, calmer orbit; a friend greeting swoops close and bouncy.

- **Why it works:** Register is felt as distance, timing, and body language—not memorized as a rule.

- **Transformation:** The silhouettes clasp hands, becoming a door-shaped human shadow.

### 3. Identitätsregister — Name, origin, language, age, contact details

- **Wall and boards:** `Ich heiße …`, `Ich komme aus …`, `Ich wohne in …`, `Ich spreche …`, `Ich bin … Jahre alt`, and `Meine Nummer ist …`. Boards are portraits, country textures, city postcards, language sound-art, birthday candles, and phone-number strips.

- **New interaction — Elastic-ink passport:** A blank copper passport floats in the room. Punkt bounces boards through physical phrase moulds: a country must launch out of a map cavity for `aus`; a city lands inside a pin for `in`; a language passes a mouth-shaped press. Number boards land in spoken beats, not visual digit order.

- **Why it works:** A personal introduction is physically printed as one continuous moving identity strip.

- **Transformation:** The completed profile folds itself into a paper plane and flies through the next wall.

### 4. Artikelarchiv — Nouns, gender, plurals, `ein/eine`, `kein/nicht`

- **Wall and boards:** `der Tisch` gets a table board, `die Lampe` a lamp, `das Buch` a book, `zwei Bücher` a blooming book group, `Das ist kein Ball` an erased-ball board, and `Der Ball ist nicht rot` a blue-ball board.

- **New interaction — Article carapace:** Every noun board begins bare. Three living plaster ribbons—`der`, `die`, `das`—can wrap around it as a tactile shell. The correct shell holds; the wrong one harmlessly wrinkles off. Passing a singular board through a “many” rain curtain makes it physically bloom into its German plural pattern.

- **Why it works:** Articles and plurals become inseparable properties of a noun. The material behavior is a memory device, never a fake claim that German gender has logical meaning.

- **Transformation:** The three plaster streams braid into an archway; plural boards become skylights above it.

### 5. Kabinett der geliehenen Schatten — Pronouns, `sein`, `haben`, family, possession

- **Wall and boards:** `Das ist meine Schwester`, `Du bist …`, `Er hat ein Auto`, `Wir haben einen Hund`, `Ihre Mutter …`. Boards are family portraits, a dog, a bicycle, an apartment key, and paired relationships.

- **New interaction — Borrowed-shadow relay:** A rolling lantern detaches Punkt’s shadow and lets it inhabit a person, a group, or an object. Looking through the shadow creates `ich`; facing it creates `du`; observing creates `er/sie/es`; linking shadows creates `wir/ihr/sie`. Possessive boards only stabilize when viewed from the right person’s perspective.

- **Why it works:** Pronouns are roles the player inhabits, rather than a pronoun table.

- **Transformation:** The lantern mirror stops reflecting Punkt and reveals the next chamber instead.

### 6. Haus von Hier — Home, furniture, `es gibt`, locations and prepositions

- **Wall and boards:** `Es gibt eine Küche`, `Das Buch ist auf dem Tisch`, `Die Katze ist unter dem Bett`, `Der Stuhl ist neben der Tür`, `Im Bad …`. Boards depict furniture, rooms, pets, and everyday objects.

- **New interaction — Foldspace house:** The player pinches a corner of realistic wallpaper and folds the bare chamber into a tiny physical dollhouse. Boards roll under real gravity as the house changes shape. A board snaps only during the exact moment its German relation is true.

- **Why it works:** `auf`, `unter`, `neben`, and `in` become discoveries about spatial reality, not labels beneath a diagram.

- **Transformation:** The miniature house unfolds into a full-scale staircase through the wall.

### 7. Wegfindungs-Atrium — City places, transit, directions

- **Wall and boards:** `Wo ist der Bahnhof?`, `geradeaus`, `links`, `rechts`, `zur Apotheke`, `ins Café`, `nach Hause`. Boards show a station sign, pharmacy cross, tram stop, café, and street corners.

- **New interaction — Echo masonry:** Direction boards are acoustic bricks. Throwing or rotating one redirects a visible ring of sound around the chamber. The player builds a temporary echo-route while listening to German instructions in timed chunks, then rides the final sound ring to the destination.

- **Why it works:** Directions are learned as reflected sound and momentum, not map pins or floor arrows.

- **Transformation:** The last echo hits the wall like a tuning fork and cracks open a city-shaped passage.

### 8. Zahlenschwarm-Sonnenuhr — Numbers, dates, prices, clock time

- **Wall and boards:** `Es ist sieben Uhr`, `am Montag`, `um acht Uhr`, `von zehn bis zwölf`, `einundzwanzig Euro`, and simple dates. Boards depict clocks, calendars, tickets, price tags, and appointments.

- **New interaction — Reverse number river:** Number boards skim across a dry circular sound basin. For `einundzwanzig`, the “one” bead must hop through an `und` wake before it joins `zwanzig`, physically encoding German number reversal. A transparent time-lens casts correct clock hands and day bands onto the board.

- **Why it works:** The strange German number order is experienced as a satisfying motion sequence.

- **Transformation:** The number-beads fuse into a giant sunlit clock hand that becomes a bridge.

### 9. Lebendiger Verbmotor — Present tense, V2 order, separable verbs

- **Wall and boards:** `Ich stehe um sieben Uhr auf`, `Heute lerne ich Deutsch`, `Er fährt zur Arbeit`, `Wir gehen einkaufen`, `Ich rufe meine Mutter an`. Boards are daily-life scenes.

- **New interaction — Verb braid:** Scene boards split into elastic threads for subject, time, verb, object, and place. One smooth lasso around a verb spindle creates the sentence. The second beat must be the finite verb; if `Heute` begins the sentence, the verb physically vaults into second position. Prefixes such as `auf-` and `an-` fly away as little boomerangs and return at the end.

- **Why it works:** German word order becomes something the player can feel holding tension in their hands.

- **Transformation:** Finished sentence braids zip the concrete wall apart.

### 10. Verb-Biss-Café — Food, drink, polite requests, basic accusative

- **Wall and boards:** `Ich möchte einen Kaffee`, `Ich nehme die Suppe`, `Haben Sie Wasser?`, `Kein Zucker, bitte`, `Ich esse gern Brot`. Boards show food in tactile editorial-photo style.

- **New interaction — The verb bite:** A bouncy verb-frame acts like a harmless mechanical mouth. When it “acts on” a food board, the article skin stretches visibly: `ein` becomes `einen`; `der` becomes `den`. A soft politeness wind changes the action into `möchte` and `bitte`.

- **Why it works:** Accusative is not introduced as a chart. The learner sees and feels that an action changes the thing it touches.

- **Transformation:** Each correct order prints as a curling wall receipt; steam etches a door handle into tile.

### 11. Maßmarkt — Shopping, quantities, clothing, sizes, prices

- **Wall and boards:** `Wie viel kostet das?`, `Ich nehme zwei Kilo Äpfel`, `eine Flasche Wasser`, `Haben Sie das in Größe M?`, `Das kostet zwölf Euro`. Boards show apples, bottles, shirts, shoes, and price art.

- **New interaction — Resonance scale:** Goods float over a stretched sound-membrane. A quantity frame changes their physical mass: `Kilo` expands apples into a weighted cluster; `Flasche` pulls water into a bottle; `Größe M` reshapes a clothing board. The price is felt as a repeatable beat pattern before it becomes visible.

- **Why it works:** Shopping language becomes a tiny performance of weight, measure, and spoken rhythm.

- **Transformation:** The scale membrane folds into a giant shopping bag, whose opening is the exit.

### 12. Versprechenskammer — Invitations, preferences, modals, availability

- **Wall and boards:** `Kannst du heute kommen?`, `Ich kann am Freitag kommen`, `Ich möchte ins Kino gehen`, `Ich muss arbeiten`, `Leider kann ich nicht`, `Wollen wir …?`. Boards show cinema, park, calendar, phone, and work scenes.

- **New interaction — Possibility foam:** Activity boards are little gliders in dense foam. `können` creates lift, `wollen` creates a directional gust, `möchten` softly pulls, and `müssen` creates gravity. The infinitive stays tethered at the far end of the room, making its final sentence position visible.

- **Why it works:** Modal verbs change what the player can physically do, so ability, desire, and obligation feel genuinely different.

- **Transformation:** Impossible flight paths knit into a glowing bridge.

### 13. Wetterkörper — Weather, clothing, body, wellbeing, help

- **Wall and boards:** `Es ist kalt`, `Es regnet`, `Ich bin müde`, `Ich habe Kopfschmerzen`, `Mir ist schlecht`, `Ich brauche Hilfe`, `Ich brauche eine Jacke`. Boards show rain, seasons, clothing, and expressive character portraits.

- **New interaction — Thermal truth:** Boards have haptic “temperatures” and textures: wet, cold, heavy, warm, scratchy. The player presses them against Punkt or a wall silhouette, transferring the physical condition into a phrase-path. A correct German response creates warmth, shelter, or a help-signal.

- **Why it works:** It is not dress-up. The player first reads physical evidence, then expresses a real need.

- **Transformation:** The room’s cold concrete shifts to believable warm daylight; rain becomes a luminous doorway.

### 14. Echo Exit — Repair language and living conversation

- **Wall and boards:** The wall begins with `Wie bitte?`, `Noch einmal, bitte`, `Bitte langsam`, `Was bedeutet …?`, and `Ich verstehe nicht`. Every earlier board returns, now mixed into new contexts.

- **New interaction — Conversational murmuration:** The restored boards lift from the walls as a flock. A wall character speaks a slow German need—a route, a coffee, an appointment, a health issue—and pulls relevant boards into motion. The player steers a phrase-flock into a response, speaks it or traces its rhythm, then offers it to the character. If they miss meaning, a repair phrase visibly rewinds the spoken sound into a slower ribbon.

- **Why it works:** It is not a dialogue tree. Several contextually valid German replies work, and the player must recover from misunderstanding.

- **Transformation:** Five randomized life scenes resolve into the copper postcard. Punkt jumps into the dot over the `i` in **Ich bin hier**, and the house opens onto an ordinary German street: the beginning of A1.

## Why this stays fast and beautiful

The rooms are 20–25 metre minimalist boxes: realistic concrete, plaster, tile, oxidized metal, or painted walls with baked lighting. Only Punkt and 6–10 active boards exist at once.

- Rounded, squashy character and props provide the playful physics.
- Walls use texture atlases, decals, shader-driven fog/ripples, and baked transformations rather than heavy geometry.
- Doorways conceal loading, so only one chamber is active.
- Every board is one reusable instanced object with different art and material behavior.
- One-thumb grab, aim, and release keeps it mobile-native; speech recognition is optional, never a gate.

The addictive loop is: discover a strange physical law, earn the perfect magnetic wall-snap, watch a dead room regain a sliver of life, and see another piece of the postcard reveal why Punkt must keep going.
