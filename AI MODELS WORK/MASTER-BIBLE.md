# VOLUME I: SYSTEMIC GAME ARCHITECTURE & WORLD MECHANICS

## 1.1 — The Macro-Narrative Clock: The 90-Day Visa Countdown
The central driving engine of the early game is not an abstract "level meter" or an arbitrary quest marker. It is the cold, legal reality of **The 90-Day Tourist Visa**.
- **Day 0:** Alex Herrera lands at Flughafen Berlin Brandenburg (BER) with a stamped Mexican passport granting exactly 90 calendar days of Schengen tourist presence.
- **The Legal Clock:** Every in-game morning, the HUD subtly displays:
  `Tag [X] / 90 Tage bis zum Visa-Ablauf` (e.g., *Tag 1 / Noch 89 Tage gültig*).
- **The Mandatory Legal Chain (Strict Dependencies):**
  1. **Die Anmeldung (Days 1–14 Deadline):** By federal German law (§ 17 BMG), any resident moving into a home must register at the Bürgeramt within 14 days. Failure triggers formal administrative warnings (*Mahnungen*) and fines starting at €50 up to €1,000.
  2. **Die Wohnungsgeberbestätigung:** Cannot register at the Bürgeramt without this specific landlord document signed by the building owner (Herr Brandt).
  3. **Das Girokonto (Days 15–25):** Cannot receive legal wages or set up rent *Lastschrift* (direct debit) without a verified German IBAN from Sparkasse or Deutsche Bank. Requires *Meldebescheinigung* from step 1.
  4. **Die Krankenversicherung (Days 25–40):** Mandatory statutory health insurance (*Gesetzliche Krankenversicherung* via Techniker Krankenkasse/TK). Requires German address and bank details.
  5. **Der Arbeitsvertrag / Minijob-Nachweis (Days 40–70):** Proof of employment or language student status required for the permit application.
  6. **Der Aufenthaltstitel-Antrag (Days 75–89):** The bureaucratic climax at the *Landesamt für Einwanderung* (LEA) on Friedrich-Krause-Ufer.
- **Failure State Mechanics:** If the timer reaches Day 90 without a booked appointment or submitted dossier, the status does not trigger an arcade "Game Over". Instead, the world shifts to **Illegal Overstay Crisis Mode**: bank cards lock, police checks at U-Bahn stations become terrifying stealth/evasion encounters, and the narrative branches into emergency legal repair paths via refugee/migrant legal clinics (*Migrationsberatung*).

## 1.2 — The Real Resource Economy: The €2,400 Starting Capital
The player starts with **€2,400.00** in savings, visually represented on their smartphone banking app (*N26/Sparkasse* interface).
- **Fixed Monthly Expenses (Recurring Outflows):**
  - Room Sublet (*WG-Zimmer Miete* in Neukölln): **€480.00 / month** (due on the 1st of every month).
  - Utility/Internet Share (*Nebenkosten & WLAN*): **€65.00 / month**.
  - BVG Monthly Transit Ticket (*Berlin AB Monatskarte*): **€86.00 / month**.
  - Statutory Health Insurance Student/Jobseeker Rate: **€120.00 / month** (begins month 2).
- **Variable Daily Living Expenses:**
  - Standard bakery roll (*Schrippe / Brötchen*): **€0.35 – €0.45**.
  - Morning filter coffee (*Kaffee zum Mitnehmen*): **€2.20 – €2.80**.
  - Street Döner Kebab at Mustafa’s Imbiss: **€4.50 – €6.00**.
  - Weekly supermarket groceries (REWE/Aldi basic subsistence): **€35.00 – €55.00**.
  - Basement laundry machine coin (*Waschmünze*): **€2.00 / load**.
- **Financial Pressure Curve:** At basic subsistence without income, the player will exhaust €2,400 within approximately 70–80 days. Finding legal part-time employment (*Minijob bis 520€*) by Act II is not an optional side quest; it is a biological and economic prerequisite for narrative survival.

# VOLUME II: DRAMATIS PERSONAE (COMPLETE CHARACTER DOSSIERS)

## 2.1 — The Protagonist: Alex Herrera
- **Age:** 29.
- **Origin:** Guadalajara, Jalisco, Mexico.
- **Background & Physicality:** Professional sous-chef with 8 years of brutal, high-speed line-cook experience. Lean, broad-shouldered, with prominent burn scars spanning from the wrist to mid-forearm on the left arm (from a boiling stockpot incident in 2020). Hands are permanently calloused, fingers possessing high dexterity.
- **Psychological Profile:** Highly competent, proud, used to commanding respect in kitchens. Plunging into Germany strips him of his voice, reducing an expert adult to the verbal status of a toddler. This creates a specific, intense interior tension: **The Dignity Struggle**. Alex refuses to be baby-talked or pitied.
- **The Core Emotional Anchor (The Tomás Photograph):**
  - Kept in the inner pocket of his canvas jacket: a faded, creased black-and-white print taken in October 1968 outside the Siemens-Schuckertwerke in West Berlin.
  - It shows his maternal great-uncle, Tomás Navarro, standing in work overalls beside an Opel Kadett, smiling broadly with grease on his cheek.
  - Tomás vanished from family records in 1971 after sending his final money transfer to Guadalajara. The family folklore claimed he "disappeared into the cold German air."
  - This photograph sits on Alex’s nightstand throughout the entire game. In Acts I & II, Alex does not possess enough German to formulate questions about him. In Act III, it becomes the catalyst for investigative visits to the *Landesarchiv Berlin*.

## 2.2 — The Shadow Companion: Lena Marie Vogel
- **Age:** 28.
- **Origin:** Freiburg im Breisgau (Baden-Württemberg). Relocated to Berlin in 2017.
- **Residence:** Apartment 3B, 3. Obergeschoss (one floor directly above Alex’s flat).
- **Profession:** Certified DaF (*Deutsch als Fremdsprache*) integration course instructor at the Volkshochschule (VHS) Neukölln, supplemented by freelance technical translation (German-English-Spanish).
- **Visual Design:** Wears oversized moss-green chunky knit wool sweaters, straight-leg dark denim, round thin-rimmed antique brass spectacles, and brown leather Blundstone boots. Dark blonde hair messily pinned up with a wooden chopstick. Carries a weathered leather messenger bag containing red-ink pens, integration course workbooks, and an insulated Thermos flask of ginger tea.
- **Linguistic Mannerisms:**
  - Speaks crisp, articulate Standard German (*Hochdeutsch*), but under fatigue or emotional intensity, subtle southwestern Alemannic cadences emerge (diminutive *-le*, e.g., "Guck mal das Zettel**le** an").
  - Unconscious bilingual habit: Speaks German aloud while quietly translating key nouns under her breath in English or Spanish.
- **The Four Facial Expression Animation States (Pedagogical Telemetry):**
  1. **The Thinking Squint (*Der Denker-Blick*):** Head cocked 15 degrees right, eyebrows drawn slightly together, lips pursed. Triggered when the player makes a near-miss error (e.g., wrong article or case). Signifies: *"I see what you're trying to say; try to self-repair before I help."*
  2. **The Proud Beam (*Das stolze Strahlen*):** Eyes crinkle deeply behind gold frames, lips press together in a wide, restrained smile, accompanied by a single, deliberate downward nod. Triggered when the player successfully produces an unassisted, grammatically complex sentence in a public space.
  3. **The Warning Tilt (*Der Mahnungs-Kipp*):** Eyebrows raise high, chin drops, eyes widen over the rims of her glasses. Triggered when the player is about to commit a major cultural or bureaucratic transgression (e.g., using informal *du* with a police officer or crossing a red pedestrian light in front of children).
  4. **The Protective Shield (*Die Schutzwand*):** Steely, flat, unblinking neutral stare directed at rude or impatient NPCs. She steps forward by half a foot, creating physical and social space for the player to breathe and gather words.

## 2.3 — The Roommate: Tom Müller
- **Age:** 31.
- **Origin:** Munich, Bavaria.
- **Residence:** The primary leaseholder of the 3-room Altbau WG flat on Weserstraße.
- **Profession:** Senior UI/UX Designer at a fintech startup in Kreuzberg; works hybrid from the kitchen table.
- **Personality & Function:** Easygoing, chronically untidy, plays an acoustic guitar on the sofa at 22:30, drinks Augustiner beer from the bottle.
- **Pedagogical Role:** Tom is the **Anti-Lena**. He is not an educator and has no patience for textbook pedantry. He speaks natural, rapid, slang-infused Berlin/Munich youth German: *"Ey Alter, hast du Bock auf Döner?"*, *"Krass, die Waschmaschine spinnt schon wieder."* He forces the player to decode mumbled speech, contractions, and modern colloquialisms.

## 2.4 — The Cultural & Bureaucratic Antagonists/Allies
- **Herr Dietrich Krüger (72):** Retired high school history teacher living on the 2nd floor. Formal, impeccable, cardigan-wearing, protector of building rules (*Hausordnung*). Demands strict observance of quiet hours (*Ruhezeit: 22:00–07:00 and all day Sunday*). The benchmark for formal *Sie*-register and historical depth.
- **Frau Hannelore Schneider (54):** Master baker at *Bäckerei Schneider*. Floured apron, short auburn perm, sharp blue eyes. Runs her shop at breakneck speed. Tolerates zero hesitation during morning rush hour (07:30–08:30). Becoming accepted by Frau Schneider is the ultimate early-game social triumph.
- **Mustafa Ceylan (48):** Owner of the corner Döner Imbiss. Turkish-German, living in Neukölln for 30 years. Warm, boisterous, addresses regular customers as *"Chef"* or *"Habibi"*. Models authentic Berlin multi-ethnic street dialect (*Kiezdeutsch*).
- **Herr Klaus Brandt (68):** The landlord and building owner. Gruff, cigarette-stained fingers, communicates via grunts and brief, terrifying legal terms. The gatekeeper to the essential *Wohnungsgeberbestätigung*.
- **Chef Katarina Weber (34):** Head chef and owner of *Restaurante Raíces*, a high-end 24-seat fusion restaurant in Mitte. Alex's former culinary colleague in Mexico. Demanding, passionate, switches between rapid-fire kitchen Spanish and clipped, authoritative professional German commands (*"Zwei Entenbrust auf Drei, schick das Gemüse jetzt, Zack-Zack!"*).

# VOLUME V: THE BRIDGE — DAYS 2 THROUGH 14
### *The 13-Day Scaffolding Dissolution Protocol*

To ensure absolute design integrity, the game does not magically jump from Day 1 to Day 15. The scaffolding is systematically dismantled across Days 2–14 in accordance with this exact pedagogical curve:

```
SCAFFOLDING LEVEL (%)
100% |===== [Day 1: Full Lena Shadowing]
 80% |       \==== [Days 2-4: Lena waits outside shops]
 60% |             \==== [Days 5-7: First solo errands in Kiez]
 40% |                   \==== [Days 8-11: Roommate Tom arrives, chaos begins]
 20% |                         \==== [Days 12-14: Written notes only, remote texts]
  0% +------------------------------------------------------------------------>
     D1    D3    D5    D7    D9    D11    D13    D15 [FULL SOLO DAY]
```

- **Day 2 (The Repeat):** Player must execute the morning coffee and bakery run with Lena walking 5 paces behind in silence. If player looks back in panic, Lena gestures forward: *"Du weißt wie das geht. Geh."*
- **Day 3 (The Späti Water Run):** Buying 6-pack mineral water at Mustafa's Späti. Introduction of `mit Kohlensäure` (sparkling) vs. `ohne Kohlensäure / still` (still).
- **Day 4 (First Encounter with Herr Krüger):** Meeting the retired teacher on the stairs. Krüger introduces himself using formal `Sie`. Player must navigate the polite honorific greeting without using casual `du`.
- **Day 5 (The Broken Table Leg):** Fixing the wobbly kitchen table. Visiting the local hardware store (*Bauhaus*) to buy wood glue (`Holzleim`) and sandpaper (`Schleifpapier`). First encounter with technical nouns.
- **Day 6 (Solo Bakery Milestone):** Lena sends a morning text: *"Bin 20 Minuten später. Holst du schon Brötchen?"* Alex completes his first 100% unassisted solo transaction with Frau Schneider. Achievement Unlocked: `Allein bestellt`.
- **Day 7 (The Trash Schedule):** Müllabfuhr day. Decoding the complex color-coded municipal trash calendar (*Müllplan*) pinned on the hallway corkboard. Dragging the heavy wheeled bins into the courtyard.
- **Day 8 (The Arrival of Tom Müller):** Flatmate Tom returns from Munich. Loud, energetic, acoustic guitar case, bags of dirty laundry. Introduces colloquial German: *"Moin! Du bist Alex, oder? Cool, Mann. Alles fit?"* The linguistic comfort zone is shattered.
- **Day 9 (The U-Bahn Induction):** Riding the U8 from Hermannplatz to Alexanderplatz with Lena. Navigating the touchscreen ticket automaton (*BVG Einzelfahrschein AB*), validating the paper ticket at the red stamping post (*Entwerter* - *"Ohne Stempel bist du Schwarzfahrer!"*).

- **Day 11 (First Telephone Call):** Ordering a delivery pizza via phone. Zero visual cues; pure compressed auditory parsing. Lena sits across the table writing prompt words on a notepad.

- **Day 13 (The Handwritten Grocery List):** Decoding Tom’s messy cursive handwriting on a shopping note left on the counter. Separable verbs in notes: *"Bring Milch mit!"*

Der war echt teuer."* Alex must formulate an authentic apology in German: *"Entschuldigung, Tom. Das wusste ich nicht. Ich kaufe neuen."*

# VOLUME IX: THE CLIMAX & THE ENDING (DAY 360)

## 9.1 — The Narrative Resolution of the Tomás B-Plot

## 9.2 — The Final Scene: Mirroring Day 1
On Day 360, exactly one year after stepping out of Tegel/BER airport, Alex walks down the Weserstraße sidewalk.
- It is early March. A light drizzle falls, exactly as on Day 1.
- But everything has shifted:
  - The street is no longer an incomprehensible wall of violent sound.
  - The bakery bell chimes: Frau Schneider waves from behind the counter, calling out: *"Morgen, Alex! Dein Roggenbrot steht schon bereit!"*
  - Alex smiles, replying with casual ease: *"Danke, Hannelore, ich hol's auf dem Rückweg ab!"*

- She looks up at Alex, her eyes wide with foreign panic.
- Alex stops. He looks at her. He remembers his own first morning on this exact stone landing.
- He does not rush. He does not overwhelm her with fast words.
- Alex takes half a step forward, softens his voice, and says with warm, steady clarity:
  > *"Hallo. Guten Morgen. Ich heiße Alex. Kann ich dir helfen?"*
- The young woman stares, her lips trembling as she tries to find words in a language she does not yet own.
- Alex waits. He does not interrupt. He gives her all the space in the world to begin.
- The camera slowly pulls back up the stairwell.
- **Fade to Black.**

   - Decay curves accelerate if words are not naturally embedded in daily physical routines.
2. **Dynamic NPC Speech Parsing & Modulation:**
   - NPCs dynamically adjust speech rate (syllables/sec) and syntactic complexity based on the player’s real-time CEFR telemetry score.
   - When player fatigue or error rates spike (>3 consecutive communication breakdowns), NPCs don't drop to English; they execute naturalistic communicative repair (slowing cadence, using gestures, pointing, recasting with simpler vocabulary).
3. **Voice Input & Pronunciation Scoring:**
   - Speech-to-text pipeline analyzes phonemic accuracy, with targeted feedback on challenging German phonetic boundaries:
     - The glottal stop (*Knacklaut*).
     - Front rounded vowels (*ö*, *ü*).

     - Final consonant devoicing (*Auslautverhärtung*).
4. **Failure State Philosophy:**
   - No death. No Game Over screens.
   - Every failure is a narrative scene. If you buy salt instead of sugar, your morning coffee tastes atrocious, Alex grimaces, and the game asks: *"Was hast du falsch gelesen?"* Life simply moves forward, teaching you through its consequences.
